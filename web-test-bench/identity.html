ARCHITECTURE SPECIFICATION · FUTURE WORK

One identity.
A verifiable history.

Node Core OS proposes building an evidence history to evaluate identity constancy, record personhood checks, and present specific claims without revealing the entire private history.

Explore the model ↓ Read the full specification ↗
Transparency note: this page describes a proposed architecture. It does not claim that personhood verification, distributed consensus, or zero-knowledge proofs are implemented.

01 · CORE MODEL

Two different questions

Constancy builds evidence of activity. Personhood requires a specific verification procedure. These concepts can be related, but they are not equivalent.

A · PERSONHOOD VERIFICATION

Does the identity correspond to a real person?

A versioned procedure must define what is checked, how the result is bound to the identity, how long the verification remains valid, and what safeguards it provides against impersonation and duplicate identities.

Planned
B · CONSTANCY

Does it maintain a valid history?

Protocol-defined activity, eligible voting, recognized propagation, and accepted records can provide evidence of continuity across defined periods.

Planned
Safety rule: a technically active identity, an ordinary vote, or a long chain does not by itself prove that a unique, real person is behind it. Any such conclusion must be limited to the guarantees of an explicit verification procedure.

02 · EVIDENCE LIFECYCLE

From activity to recognition

This flow is conceptual; each stage requires schemas, deterministic rules, and tests before it can be considered operational.

01IdentityA technical reference exists and its binding is defined.
02ActivityRecords are generated for actions that satisfy the rules.
03PropagationThe peers or checkpoints that observed the evidence are verified.
04Validation and consensusRecords are recognized only under a defined and tested protocol.
05HistoryValid references, periods, renewals, and gaps accumulate.

03 · LONGEST CHAIN

A constancy reference, not automatic truth

The longest valid history may serve as a temporal comparison reference after its records and acceptance under network rules have been validated.

Illustrative example10 años

History válido más largo reconocido según una política determinada.

Descriptive metric7 / 10 = 70%

A seven-year history equals 70% of that temporal reference. It does not mean 70% trust or a 70% probability of being a real person.

  • Measure duration, valid periods, continuity, gaps, freshness, count, and propagation separately.
  • Do not inflate constancy with large files, duplicate records, or repeated votes.
  • Do not count a missing period as continuous activity.
  • Do not treat a history as true solely because it is the longest; validation, conflict-resolution, and finality rules are required.
  • A long chain does not replace personhood verification or automatically validate other credentials.

04 · VOTING AND CONSENSUS

Different functions

A vote may provide participation evidence or form part of a specific procedure. Consensus recognizes network state and does not have to be a simple majority.

Preferences

A preference vote records a choice and produces a collective result under published rules. The selected option must not change the individual participation evidence.

Personhood check

It may require a challenge or a credential with defined guarantees. An ordinary vote is not, by itself, proof of personhood.

Consenso

Recognizes records or states according to the algorithm, participant model, fault assumptions, and finality rules.

Failing to respond to a check does not automatically prove fraud. Pending, renewal, grace-period, review, and recovery states are needed.

06 · SELECTIVE DISCLOSURE

Prove a condition without revealing everything

Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs) may allow a specific property of a credential to be demonstrated without disclosing the complete document, where the credential and cryptographic protocol support it.

What remains private

The full personal record, original credential, or sensitive data, preferably in user-controlled or encrypted storage.

→

What is presented

A minimal proof of the required claim, bound to the verifier, purpose, challenge, and freshness requirements to reduce misuse.

  • A ZKP does not make a false claim true or automatically make an issuer trustworthy.
  • Verification must account for credential validity and revocation where applicable.
  • A relying service should request only the information necessary for its purpose.

07 · PRIVACY AND STORAGE

Storing does not mean publishing

Local storage and Kubo/IPFS serve complementary roles. A CID identifies content, but does not provide confidentiality or control who can obtain it.

Local or encrypted

Preferred for personal sources, family, intimate, or health information, and material that should not be disclosed. Keys must be managed separately.

Distributed and public

Reserved for content intended to be shared. Public manifests should not reveal sensitive labels, private references, or personal data by default.

Unpublishing a reference does not guarantee deletion of copies already distributed through IPFS. The website must not promise confidentiality or deletion that the infrastructure cannot guarantee.

08 · IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

Build in verifiable phases

Implementation should progress from simple local schemas and deterministic tests toward cryptography and distributed networks, without claiming capabilities before they are tested.

  1. Specification and threat modelDefine personhood verification, its assurance level, privacy, and limitations.
  2. Local schemasSeparate activity records, personhood checks, and individual credentials.
  3. Deterministic validationTest timestamps, periods, duplicates, gaps, validity, renewal, and revocation.
  4. Constancy metricsCalculate duration and continuity only from valid records, showing provenance and missing periods.
  5. Preference simulationSeparate the collective tally from individual evidence and protect private choices.
  6. Propagation y consenso multinodoDefine peer authentication, Sybil resistance, fault model, and finality before implementation.
  7. Credential catalogApply issuer-, integrity-, scope-, privacy-, and revocation-specific rules.
  8. Zero-knowledge proofsSelect reviewed protocols and test verifier binding, challenges, freshness, and revocation.
  9. Third-party verificationProvide explainable, contextual results rather than a universal trust score.
