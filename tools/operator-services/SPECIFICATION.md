# Operator Services and Compensation — Product Specification

**Status:** Future product proposal. No income or payment capability is implied by this document.

## Intended service

Enable an operator or identity holder to complete a customer-requested task and receive compensation when the task meets documented acceptance criteria. Examples to evaluate include credential verification, authorized attestations, availability commitments, content distribution, audits, and other measurable services.

## Required contract for every paid task

- Customer and service provider roles.
- Task scope, prerequisites, price or reward rule, and deadline.
- Evidence required to demonstrate completion.
- Acceptance, rejection, appeal and dispute process.
- Fees, payout method, settlement timing, and failed-payment handling.
- Abuse/fraud controls, accounting, privacy, tax and jurisdictional responsibilities.
- Clear statement of whether the payment is fiat, a digital asset, or another legally reviewed instrument.

## Economic principles

- Compensation must be funded by real customer demand or a clearly disclosed and sustainable funding source.
- Node registration, node ownership, uptime alone, recruitment, or token holding must not be represented as guaranteed income.
- One operator may run multiple nodes, but node count alone must not multiply human governance rights or create duplicate rewards for the same work.
- Do not promise rewards before the service, funding, acceptance and settlement rules have been tested.
- Custody of customer funds and participant funds must be explicitly assigned and reviewed before launch.

## Acceptance tests before launch

Demonstrate task assignment, duplicate-submission resistance, evidence validation, acceptance/rejection, transparent fee calculation, dispute handling, payment failure handling, and auditable accounting. Obtain appropriate legal and security review before handling public payments.
